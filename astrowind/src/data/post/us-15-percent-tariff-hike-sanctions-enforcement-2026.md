## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What exactly is a "tariff" and how does it affect my business?**
A: A tariff is essentially a tax or duty that a government levies on imported goods when they enter the country. If a 15% tariff is applied to your products, it means you'll pay an additional 15% of the value of those goods to US Customs and Border Protection (CBP), directly increasing your cost to acquire and sell those items.

**Q: What are "sanctions" and why do I need to screen for them?**
A: Sanctions are restrictions placed by governments on specific countries, entities (like companies), or individuals to pressure them into changing their policies, often for national security or foreign policy reasons. You need to screen your business partners because it is illegal for US persons and businesses to engage in transactions with sanctioned parties, and unknowingly doing so can lead to massive fines and serious legal trouble.

**Q: How can I check if my business partners or customers are on a sanctions list?**
A: The most effective and reliable way is to use specialized sanctions screening software, like TradeShield AI, which automatically checks against frequently updated global sanctions lists. While you can manually check official government lists from agencies like OFAC, these lists update constantly, making manual checks prone to human error and difficult to manage for high volumes.

**Q: What happens if I accidentally violate sanctions or tariffs?**
A: For sanctions violations, penalties can be extremely severe, including civil fines of hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars per violation, criminal charges, and revocation of your export privileges. For tariff violations, it could mean delayed shipments, additional duties, penalties, or even seizure of your goods, alongside potential audits from customs authorities.

**Q: Will this 15% global tariff hike affect my products from Europe or China?**
A: Yes, if the "global" tariff hike goes through as reported, it would likely apply to imports from nearly all countries, including those in Europe and China. This means that products you source from these regions for import into the US would also be subject to the additional 15% tax upon entering the United States.
