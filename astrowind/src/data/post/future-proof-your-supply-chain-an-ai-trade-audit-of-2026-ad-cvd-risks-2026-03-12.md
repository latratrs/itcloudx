## Overview

As we look toward 2026, the landscape of U.S. trade compliance is increasingly defined by aggressive enforcement of Antidumping and Countervailing Duty (AD/CVD) laws. These trade remedies, designed to shield domestic industries from unfairly priced imports and foreign government subsidies, are being wielded with greater frequency and impact. Recent petitions and final determinations, such as those concerning van-type trailers from Canada, Mexico, and China, and battery-grade graphite from China, serve as critical indicators of this trend. For U.S. importers, ignoring these developments is not an option. A passive approach can lead to crippling retroactive duties, disrupted supply chains, and significant financial penalties. Proactive risk assessment, including the use of advanced AI-powered trade audits, is now essential for navigating this complex and high-stakes environment.

## Key Developments

The **U.S. Department of Commerce** and the **U.S. International Trade Commission (ITC)** are currently managing a docket of high-profile AD/CVD cases that directly impact major industrial and emerging technology sectors. Understanding these cases is crucial for forecasting future compliance risks.

One of the most significant recent actions is the AD/CVD petition filed by the Coalition of American Trailer Manufacturers. This petition targets van-type trailers and their subassemblies imported from Canada, Mexico, and China. The petitioners allege substantial dumping margins, with claims exceeding **100%** for some Chinese producers. The **Commerce Department** has initiated investigations, and the **ITC** is conducting its preliminary injury determination. If these investigations result in affirmative findings, importers could face steep cash deposit requirements on future entries and potential retroactive liability.

Simultaneously, the **Commerce Department** has issued a final affirmative determination in its AD/CVD investigation of certain graphite products from China. This decision imposes significant duties on battery-grade graphite, a critical component for electric vehicles and energy storage systems. This case highlights the administration's focus on strategic sectors and its willingness to use trade remedies to protect domestic production in key supply chains. The finalization of these duties demonstrates the full lifecycle of an AD/CVD case, from petition to enforcement, providing a clear roadmap of what trailer importers might expect.

## Enforcement Actions

The consequences of an affirmative AD/CVD determination are severe and immediate. U.S. importers of goods subject to these orders face a multi-layered enforcement regime managed by the **Commerce Department** and **U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)**. Key risks and enforcement actions include:

- **Cash Deposit Requirements:** Upon a preliminary affirmative determination, importers are required to post cash deposits with **CBP** for all subsequent entries of the subject merchandise. These deposit rates are based on the preliminary dumping or subsidy margins and can dramatically increase the landed cost of goods.
- **Retroactive Liability:** Duties can be applied retroactively to goods entered up to 90 days before the **Commerce Department's** preliminary determination if a finding of “critical circumstances” is made. This creates significant unforeseen liabilities for importers.
- **Annual Administrative Reviews:** Duty rates are not static. They are subject to annual reviews where the **Commerce Department** can adjust the rates for specific exporters. This creates long-term uncertainty, as final duty liability may not be known for years after the import transaction.
- **Scope Rulings and Anti-Circumvention:** **CBP** and **Commerce** are vigilant in policing efforts to evade AD/CVD orders through misclassification, transshipment, or minor product modifications. Importers found to be circumventing an order face substantial penalties and potential criminal investigation.

## What Importers Must Do Now

To navigate the heightened AD/CVD risks in 2026 and beyond, importers must shift from a reactive to a proactive compliance posture. Waiting for an official notice of investigation is too late. The following steps are critical for mitigating risk:

1.  **Conduct a Proactive Supply Chain Audit:** Leverage technology, including AI-powered tools, to conduct a comprehensive audit of your import portfolio. Identify products sourced from countries frequently targeted by AD/CVD petitions. Analyze your Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTSUS) classifications to determine if your goods could fall within the scope of potential or existing orders.

2.  **Verify Origin and Supplier Practices:** Scrutinize your suppliers' operations. Obtain robust documentation to verify the country of origin for all components and finished goods. Discuss pricing structures with your foreign suppliers to understand their potential exposure to dumping allegations. Inquire about any government subsidies they may receive.

3.  **Diversify Sourcing:** Over-reliance on a single country, particularly one with a history of AD/CVD cases, is a significant vulnerability. Begin researching and qualifying alternative suppliers in different regions to build resilience into your supply chain and reduce the impact of a new AD/CVD order.

4.  **Engage Legal and Trade Experts:** Consult with experienced trade counsel to understand the nuances of AD/CVD law. They can provide early warnings on potential petitions, assist in responding to **Commerce Department** questionnaires, and help prepare for scope inquiries or administrative reviews.

5.  **Budget for Contingencies:** The financial impact of AD/CVD duties can be substantial. Importers should factor potential cash deposit requirements and retroactive duty liabilities into their financial planning and pricing models to avoid catastrophic financial shocks.
