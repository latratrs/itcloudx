## The Intensifying Landscape of CBP Enforcement and UFLPA

At the heart of current U.S. trade enforcement is the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA), enacted to prevent goods made with forced labor in China's Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region (XUAR) from entering the U.S. market. The Act operates under a "rebuttable presumption" – meaning any goods produced wholly or in part in Xinjiang, or by entities identified on the UFLPA Entity List, are presumed to be made with forced labor and are prohibited from entry unless the importer can provide clear and convincing evidence to the contrary (Source 6).

This presumption places an enormous burden of proof on importers. CBP actively enforces UFLPA, and companies must be prepared to demonstrate due diligence across their entire supply chain, sometimes going many tiers deep. The focus isn't just on direct suppliers but on every component, raw material, and labor input from origin to finished product.
